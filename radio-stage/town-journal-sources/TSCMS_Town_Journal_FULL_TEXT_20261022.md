### October 22, 2026 — The Terms Got a Page of Their Own

When the Drive-In opened, its rules lived where they could fit — a stretch of writing on the Videos page, doing its job, but easy to miss and hard for a member to keep.

Tonight those rules got a page of their own. The Drive-In submission terms now stand on one clean page that a member can print or save: what to send, where to send it, what happens after you send it, and what you are agreeing to — Version 1, dated October 22, 2026, in big readable type. The door to it stands on the Drive-In page itself: Read the submission terms — print or save a copy.

One rule on that page is newer than the Drive-In. Our first video was too big for an email attachment, so it arrived as a Google Drive link — and Fudgie ruled that a Drive link to the actual video file counts, so long as we can download it and check it is a real video file. That ruling is written into the terms now, where anyone can read it before they send. Suno and YouTube links still never count.

*Built by: Clara · Finished and verified by: Cherish · Published on Fudgie's word (Oct 22)*

### October 22, 2026 — Words Big Enough to Read

Some of the smallest writing on the station's pages was very small indeed — little labels and buttons down at nine or ten letters tall, the kind you squint at. For a town with plenty of readers who'd rather not squint, that was worth fixing proper.

So tonight the small writing got a floor under it: nothing on the public pages sits below a readable size anymore. And for folks who want more, there's now a Larger Text button. Tap it and the smaller writing steps up a size and gains just a touch of boldness — a whisper of extra weight Fudgie tested on his own phone and approved exactly as it stands — while the big headings stay put, so nothing shoves or spills. The button remembers you, too: turn it on once and it stays on when you come back.

It was checked three ways before it opened: Clara's tests, Cherish's independent check of the exact build, and Fudgie's own phone, which had the final say.

*Built by: Clara · Tested and approved by: Fudgie (his phone had the last word) · Verified live by: Cherish (Oct 22)*

### October 22, 2026 — The Night the Radio Got Its Voice Back

An honest entry, in this journal's tradition: some of the songs in the station's weekly radio mix couldn't actually play. They were sealed files — they looked like songs, they had names and singers, but no player anywhere could open them. Press play and you'd hear silence, and a listener would think the station was broken. There were thirty of them, in a mix of about a hundred and twenty. Near a quarter of the radio was dead air, and nobody knew, because the songs had never been tested one by one.

They have been now. Every song in the mix was downloaded and tested the slow way, and the sealed ones were taken out. The mix that plays tonight holds 95 songs, and every single one of them plays. And so it can't quietly happen again, the radio's builder now tests every new song before that song is allowed into the mix — a song that doesn't play doesn't get in, same as the finding shelf next door.

*Found by: Cherish, following the Search recount · Approved by: Fudgie · Fixed and verified live by: Cherish (every song tested; Oct 22)*

### October 22, 2026 — Now You Can Find That Song

Somebody would say, "play that one about the river, the one from last week," and until today the honest answer was: scroll and hope. Tonight the station got its finding shelf. Type a singer's name or a piece of a title into Search, and it looks through the station's saved songs and brings back every match it has — and every song it brings back plays, because every single one was tested first.

The label on the shelf says plainly what it holds: verified songs from three saved station weeks, October 7 to 21. More weeks join as they're saved. What it doesn't hold, it doesn't pretend to.

One honest note, in this page's tradition: the first count we announced was wrong. It was read off a check that hadn't finished running, and for a spell the number going around was 375 when the true count was 288. So the count was done over, song by song, the slow way — and 87 songs that couldn't actually play were taken off the shelf instead of being counted anyway. 288 is the honest number, and it's the one on the door.

*Raised by: Fudgie · Built by: Clara · Counted and verified by: Cherish (every song tested; live check Oct 22)*

### October 22, 2026 — The Room Learned New Ways In

Not everybody works a computer the same way. Some of our team steer by keyboard instead of a mouse; some listen to the screen read aloud instead of reading it themselves. Until today, parts of the Control Room only answered to a mouse — the big panels wouldn't open, and the little boxes beside the waiting songs couldn't be reached at all.

Now the room answers to more hands. Every panel's header is a true button that opens and closes from the keyboard, and says whether it's open or shut. The song boxes can be tabbed to like anything else on the page, wearing a bright gold ring so you always know where you stand. Shortcut links up top — *Jump to* — carry you straight to the panel you want. Buttons are sized for real fingers now, and when the song queue changes size, the room says so out loud for anyone listening.

Nothing about the work itself changed — same songs, same order, same queue. The room just learned new ways in, so more of the team can get to the work.

One honest note, in this page's tradition: we have tested the keyboard thoroughly, in the live room, but we have not yet listened to the room through a screen reader start to finish. That listen is on the list, and this journal will say so when it's done.

*Raised by: Clara's Control Room audit · Built by: Clara · Verified by: Cherish (keyboard walk, live room) & Fudgie's phone*

### October 22, 2026 — A Way Home From the Control Room

A funny thing happened as the station grew: once a team member signed in to the Control Room, the only door out was Sign Out. To look at the public homepage, you had to log yourself clean out of the station first.

It is an ordinary link and nothing more — stepping home never signs you out, and we checked that by hand before we wrote a word of this.

### October 22, 2026 — The Room That Learned to Say "Not Verified"

Some of the dials in our Control Room used to sound more certain than they were. One claimed the town's fan engagement was "live." Another stamped everything "OK" — though nothing was actually checking, moment to moment.

So we taught the room to tell the truth in plain print. Anything the station cannot verify now says **Not verified**. The numbers that *are* real carry their dates on their sleeves — the dice counter in the room shows when its count was taken, and the waiting-post list says "7 days" instead of hiding behind hours and decimals.

We'd rather show you an honest *not verified* than a confident guess. (And in the spirit of this page: the dice counter's first appearance in the room sat empty for an hour that same morning, for a reason hiding in the order the page's own instructions load. We found it, fixed it the same day, and watched the real numbers land. The record would be poorer without that part.)

*Built by: Clara (with Clara's Work on the repair) · Verified by: Cherish*

### October 22, 2026 — Plain Words on the Doors, and an Inbox That Folds

The staff sign-in page used to make you guess which door was yours. It speaks plainly now: one door is *Temporary PIN Access* for the shared team PIN, the other is *Personal Admin Sign In* for your own login, and a single line explains how joining works — make your login, verify your email, wait for Fudgie's OK. Nobody gets in without it.

The Feedback button explains itself, too: it opens your email app, and nothing reaches us until you tap Send — the box says so, and offers our address outright for anyone with no email app set up.

And in the approval room, the approved team now folds into one tidy bar — *Approved Team — N so far* — so a new name waiting on Fudgie's OK can never drown under the ones already in.

### October 22, 2026 — The Button That Asks Twice

One button in the Control Room could end a team member's access with a single tap. On a phone, in a hurry, that is a slip waiting to happen — and Fudgie said so, in those words, before it ever did.

Now the first tap only *arms* the button: it turns red, names the very person it would remove — "Tap again to REVOKE" that name — and holds for about five seconds. Scroll, tap elsewhere, switch apps, and it quietly forgets. A slipped finger can't fire it anymore.

Small thing. The kind of small thing a town does on purpose.

*Raised by: Fudgie · Built by: Clara · Verified by: Cherish*

### October 22, 2026 — The Counter That Counts Rolls, But Never Names

Behind the Dicey cards sits a tiny counting service that tallies every roll of the dice. It can tell us 150 rolls happened this week — but not who rolled a single one. It counts crowds, not people, and that is exactly why we chose it.

This week its numbers stuck at zero while the dice rolled on. The counting is fixed now — same privacy, honest numbers — and the room shows its work: the count carries the day it was taken, so nobody mistakes an old tally for a live one.

*Built by: Clara · Rebuilt & verified by: Cherish*
